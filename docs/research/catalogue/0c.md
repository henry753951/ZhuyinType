## 2. Catalogue

### A. System architecture and decoding

| # | Item | Source | What it is, with numbers | ZT status | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | Statistical engine keeps candidate generation and fallback | [R] §1, §3.1 | Lexicon word decoder (historical name "Zuin"). The neural model only rescores engine candidates and never generates them | have (lattice, Viterbi, N-best) | lead: we also inject per-character v3 scores *inside* the lattice ([MODEL §6]), which they do not |
| A2 | Engine LM: interpolated bigram/unigram | [R] §3.1 | Prior = 0.7·P_bi + 0.3·P_uni; unnormalised natural-log path scores; no softmax before fusion | have (MKN bigram, chat/wiki mixture, personal n-gram) | lead: richer mixture. Their 0.7 μ is a useful sanity check against our tuning |
| A3 | Cross-commit context in the engine | [R] §3.1, [CLI] 0c-cross-eval | At composition start, mixes a BOS-conditioned prior with the last confirmed word, weight **0.075**. The weight is chosen with the 0c-cross-eval ablation over {0, 0.025, 0.05, 0.1, 0.25, 0.5, 1}, where the grid must start at 0 | have (we continue from the last two committed words, [MODEL §8.2]) | adapt: add a 0-anchored weight sweep for our continuation, instead of hard continuation |
| A4 | Frozen dictionary package for evaluation | [R] §3.1 | 241,749 surface forms; 275,049 entries; 12,011,499 bigrams. Frozen 2026-09-21 and identified by SHA-256 | have (lexicon.bin, mmap) | adopt: hash-pin the lexicon and LM in every eval report |
| A5 | Domain-adapted bigram re-estimation | [R] §3.1 | Bigrams adjusted on 7,101 news docs (2,282,618 word tokens). Minimum doc frequency 3, mix 0.2, context prior 100, BOS mix ×0.5. Probability mass of each original bigram row is preserved before quantisation | partial (we have a separate chat LM) | adapt: re-estimate our bigrams on fresh 2025–26 Taiwan text with doc-frequency ≥3 (removes one-document artefacts) |
| A6 | Candidate set is the top 8 engine full strings | [R] §3.2, [T] | At most 8 candidates in experiments; runtime supports ≤16 (manifest `max_candidates`). All candidates have the same reading length | have (top 16 sentences) | lead on breadth. Keep 16; their 8 is cheaper |
| A7 | Fusion rule | [R] §3.2, [HFs] | s(y) = s_E(y) + w·ℓθ(y\|c,r). Override the engine default only if the fused best exceeds the default by more than τ | have (engine + 1.0·log P, exact-key margin 4.0) | adapt: add an explicit **override margin τ versus the engine default** (not only versus the corrected path) and calibrate (w, τ) jointly (see C1–C4) |
| A8 | Likelihood normalised over the full vocabulary, not over candidates | [R] §3.2 | ℓ is a true LM log-prob, so candidates of equal length are compared directly | have (v2b) | — |
| A9 | Prefix-tree batching of candidates | [R] §6, [HFs] | Shares context+reading prefix and common candidate prefixes. | A10 | Retroactive re-decode while typing | [H] demo | "先出現「以為」的預覽 → 繼續輸入ㄐㄧㄥ → 變成「已經」" | have (whole-composition redecode each key) | parity |
| A11 | Incomplete or unsupported → engine | [R] §3.2 | Incomplete reading, unsupported candidate, dictionary mismatch or inference unavailable → engine default. | A12 | Single-character list ranked by sentence fluency | [U] 0.2.2 | Single-char candidates ordered by how fluent the whole sentence is after choosing them (氣 first under 空氣) | have (v2b reranks the top 24 per length with context) | parity. Verify with N7 |

## 4. Where ZhuyinType already leads (keep and publicise)

1. **Key-level typing-error correction**: KeyDecoder `timed` plus KeyChannel. Mixed realistic errors: 38 → 57 %; near-simultaneous keys: 36 → 76.5 %. 0c has nothing comparable.
2. **In-lattice neural scoring** (v3 per-character scores inside Viterbi), not only N-best reranking (theirs is ≤8 candidates).
3. **Longer and richer context**: up to 128 app chars read, 255 held for v3. Theirs: own commits only, 64 chars.
4. **Chat/encyclopedia LM mixture with context-adaptive weighting**, plus a three-tier personal n-gram with two half-lives, delete-and-retype learning, and typo-habit learning.
5. **Memory**: 29 MB resident IME (mmap) vs their ≈250 MB of models plus Python workers.
6. **Native Core ML in process** (no Python worker IPC); energy and memory reported per key.
7. **Variant-aware scoring and output** (台/臺 preference).

## 5. Comparability caveats (read before claiming "we beat 0c")

- Their headline 86 % is **case-level** accuracy on a mix that is half hard homophone short words, with dictionary-synthesised readings and exact match. Our 80–87 % numbers are **whole-sentence** accuracy on 400-sentence sets. These cannot be compared. Only a shared test with both systems replayed settles it (action 1).
- Their external-IME baseline limits Chewing and McBopomofo to ≤39−n context syllables and counts unconvertible readings as errors. This disadvantages the baselines on long spans.
- Their latency numbers are model calls on an unspecified CPU. Ours are whole-key, end-to-end, on Apple M5.
- The punctuation test uses reference characters, not post-selection text, and excludes end-of-segment marks.
